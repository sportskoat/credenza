What a free agent haul planner gives you

Free plans differ in what they hold back. Here are the real numbers on this one, and the honest point where free stops being enough.

The short answer

The planner is free. The AI has a meter.

Everything that makes the shelf a shelf costs nothing and is not counted. You can save links, group them into a haul, size an item from a chart you read yourself, attach QC photos, and open Buy in your agent, for as long as you want, without an account.

What is counted is the work that calls a model on a server. Reading a seller size chart from a screenshot, answering a question about your own shelf, and pulling the title and photo off a link all cost money per call. Those have daily numbers.

The numbers

What free includes, per day

  • 20 link resolves. Each paste that fetches a title, a price and a photo.
  • 2 chart reads. Each size chart screenshot turned into numbers.
  • 5 questions about your own shelf.
  • 4 QC photos on each item.
  • 2 hauls at a time.

The daily counters reset at midnight UTC. They are per day, not per month, so a slow week never uses up a fast one.

Not counted

The parts with no meter on them

  • Saving a link, and how many links you save.
  • Typing a size, a price or a note by hand.
  • The parcel weight estimate.
  • Pasting a Reddit comment and turning it into a list.
  • Opening Buy in your agent.
  • Taking the whole shelf out as a spreadsheet.

This is the important half. A planner that meters the saving is a planner you cannot trust with a haul, because the haul is the thing you are trying not to lose.

Is free enough

For most weeks, yes

A haul that grows over a few weeks rarely touches the ceiling. Twenty resolves a day is twenty new finds a day. Two chart reads a day covers two sellers you have not bought from before, and you only read a chart once per seller — after that the numbers are on the item.

Free stops being enough in one specific shape: a long sitting. You open a haul thread with forty links in it and work through the whole thing in one evening. That is where the counters bite, and it is the only place they do.

Honest tradeoff

Wait a day, or pay

When you hit a counter, nothing is lost and nothing is deleted. The link still saves. You just do not get the automatic title and photo until tomorrow, and you can type them in yourself in the meantime.

Paying raises the same counters rather than unlocking a different product: 1000 resolves, 100 chart reads and 200 questions a day, 12 QC photos per item, 100 hauls. Pro is $4.99 a month, or $39.99 a year. If you never hit a limit, it buys you nothing, and the page that sells it says so.

What to compare

Three questions to ask any free planner

  • Can you get your data out? A haul you cannot export is a haul you are renting. Ask before you put forty links in.
  • Does it need an account to start? Signing up before you know whether the tool helps is backwards.
  • What is free actually free of? A free tier that caps the number of saved items is a trial with a longer name.

Credenza answers those three: export to a spreadsheet at any time, no account needed to use the shelf, and no cap on saved items.

What it is not

A planner, not a store

Credenza does not sell anything, does not hold stock, and is not a place to search for sellers. It organizes links you already found and hands them to the shopping agent you already use. The money comes from Pro, and from an agent affiliate when you open Buy on a participating agent.

The numbers on this page are the ones the app enforces. If one of them changes, this page changes with it.
