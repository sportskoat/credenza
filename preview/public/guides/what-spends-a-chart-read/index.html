What spends an AI size-chart read, and what costs nothing

Free reads 2 charts a day. Pro reads 15. Most people never reach either number, because local text parsing and reopening a saved item cost nothing. This page says exactly what spends a read.

The idea

A read is one call for one item, not one photo

A size chart on a listing is usually a picture. It is a table of numbers, photographed or screenshotted, that no browser can search and no spreadsheet can import. An AI chart read is the one job that turns that picture back into numbers: the model looks at the frames, finds the rows, and returns them as text Credenza can measure you against.

The unit is the call, not the image. One card, one request, one read — however many frames rode along with it. That matters because the free plan allows 2 chart reads a day and Pro allows 15, and the natural worry is that a chart split across three photos eats three of them. It does not. Three frames go up together and come back as one answer.

The result belongs to one item. Credenza saves it on that card and never uses it for another garment from the same seller.

Free first

Three things happen before any read is spent

Credenza does not send a request the moment you open a card. It works down a ladder, and every rung above the last one costs nothing.

  • The text on your device. Many sellers type the chart into the description instead of photographing it. Credenza parses that text locally — no request leaves the device, so no read is spent, and it works with no signal at all. It reads labelled lines and simple tables, and accepts measurements between 20 cm and 250 cm so a price or a year cannot be mistaken for a chest.
  • This item's saved chart. Reopening a card never reads the same chart again. Credenza uses the result already saved on that item, which costs zero reads.
  • Your own measurements. The size suggestion is computed on your device from the chart and your saved body profile. Comparing your chest against a chart is arithmetic, not AI, and it never costs anything — storing your measurements explains what it uses.

Only when this item has no saved chart and its text has nothing usable does Credenza offer to read the photos.

What it sends

Up to 3 photos, or up to 10 album images, in one request

There are two ways a chart reaches the reader, and both count as one read.

Photos you attach. You can add up to 3 chart images to a card — a camera shot, a screenshot, or a saved image. Three is deliberate: it covers a chart split across two frames plus a retake. Each is compressed on your device before it is sent, so a large camera photo does not become a large upload.

Album images Credenza already has. If the listing was imported with a gallery, Credenza can scan up to 10 of those images for a chart without you photographing anything. It fetches them from the seller's own image host, not from a copy — turning an album into a list covers how those images arrive.

Either way it is one call. The read has 25 seconds on the server and 30 on your device before it gives up, which is generous for a photograph of a table and short enough that a stuck request does not hold the card open.

The saved result

Each item keeps its own chart

One seller can list a shirt, trousers, and a jacket. Those garments can have completely different measurements.

Credenza stores a machine-read chart on the item that supplied the photos. Reopening that card costs nothing. Opening another item never copies the first card's measurements.

A ten-item haul can spend up to ten reads when every item needs photo reading. Typed charts still cost nothing, and each successful photo result is saved once.

The saved result lives with its item. Clearing that chart removes only that item's measurements.

Careful

A miss counts. A failure does not.

Two outcomes look similar and are charged differently, so it is worth being plain about both.

A miss counts. If the model looks at your photos and answers "no size chart here", that is a read. The work was done and the answer is real — "this listing has no chart" is useful, and it cost the same as finding one. Photographing a blurry tag and hoping will spend reads.

A failure does not count. If the request times out, if the server returns an error, or if none of the album images can be fetched, nothing is charged. Not on the server and not on your device. You can try again immediately. This is a rule we had to fix rather than one we got right first: an earlier build counted on the device the moment a reply arrived, before checking whether the reply was an error, so two timeouts could spend a free user's whole day. They no longer can.

The count

2 a day free, 15 a day on Pro, reset at midnight UTC

The counter is per day and it is the same counter on both plans, only with a different ceiling. The day boundary is midnight UTC, not your local midnight, so the reset is at a fixed hour wherever you are.

Nothing carries over. An unused read on Tuesday does not make three available on Wednesday. There is no bundle to buy and no top-up: the only way to have more in one day is Pro, and Pro's 15 is a ceiling nobody organizing their own hauls is likely to meet.

When you reach the free limit, the card does not break. It keeps whatever the local text parser found and keeps every item chart already saved. Only a new cloud read is unavailable until the reset. Pricing lists this row next to the others.

In practice

How to make two a day be enough

  • Import the listing before you photograph. The local parser only sees text Credenza has. Import first and it may find the chart with no read at all.
  • Read the important items first. Each card without usable chart text can spend one read. Start with the items you plan to buy first.
  • Photograph the whole table. One clear frame of the entire chart beats three tight crops. The reader keeps original labels, including Chinese ones such as 胸围 and 衣长, so you do not need to translate before sending.
  • Save your measurements once. A chart with nobody to compare it to is just a table. The suggestion is free and instant once the profile exists.
After

What the numbers are for

A chart on its own does not decide anything. Once Credenza has the rows, it compares them against your saved measurements and suggests a size for that garment, with the fit preference for that category applied — a jacket you want roomy and a tee you want close are measured against the same body with different targets.

The numbers stay on the card, so the decision is still there weeks later when the parcel arrives and you are checking QC photos against what you expected. Reading a Weidian size chart covers what the columns mean, and tracking QC photos covers the check at the other end.

Credenza is a decision layer, not a marketplace and not a shop. It organizes the links you already found and opens Buy in the agent you already use. It does not sell anything and it does not host a catalog.
