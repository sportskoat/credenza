What this shape buys you

  • Nobody gets lost — the rail is the whole mental model, and it matches how the money actually moves.
  • Each step has one job, so each screen can be dense without being confusing.
  • Re-entrant: a haul can sit at step 2 for three weeks. The rail is a save point, not a wizard you must finish.

What it costs

  • Five screens to build and maintain.
  • Real hauls aren't in lockstep — three items are in QC while two haven't shipped from the seller. A linear rail has to tolerate mixed states, so every step needs a "not everything is here yet" mode.
  • Partial parcels mean step 4 and 5 repeat while 1–3 stay done. The rail must loop.

Why a board

  • Mixed progress is the normal state of a haul. A board renders it natively; a stepper fights it.
  • The parcel is just another column — so partial shipping and Parcel B need no new concept.
  • Counts at the top of each column are the whole status summary. No dashboard needed.

Watch out

  • QC needs a big photo viewer, so it can't really live in a column — it becomes a modal over the board.
  • Drag on desktop is fine; the same board on mobile has to become the tap-menu in the second frame.
  • Nine columns of thumbnails at 20+ items gets noisy. Needs a collapse.

Why a table

  • The thing people are actually replacing is a spreadsheet. Beat it at its own game before you get clever.
  • Bulk selection makes a 25-item haul survivable: mark eight arrived in one action.
  • Every number in one place — price, weight, storage clock, parcel — so the shipping maths is never a separate trip.
  • Sortable by weight is genuinely useful: the top three rows are usually 70% of your shipping cost.

Watch out

  • It throws away the photos, which are the reason the shelf feels good. Needs a row-hover thumbnail at minimum.
  • Reads as admin, not as the hobby. Probably the power view, not the default.

Why parcel-first

  • Shipping is the part of the hobby people get wrong and complain about. Making it the hero is a real reason to open the app.
  • Volumetric vs actual is invisible until the agent charges you. Showing both, live, is the killer detail.
  • Weight breaks turn a calculator into advice — "add 400 g free" and "don't split this" are answers, not numbers.

Watch out

  • Needs box dimensions, which the user doesn't know until the agent packs it. Ship a sensible default per item count and let them correct it.
  • Assumes the earlier stages happened somewhere. This screen is a destination, not the whole flow — pair it with 1b or 1c.

1fMechanics worth keeping whichever shell wins

Ordering

  • One paste out, not nine. Copy every link as a single block formatted for your agent's bulk-add. The reverse of the paste bar that got items in.
  • Agent is a haul-level setting, and it picks the rate table, the referral link and the storage window. Changing agent mid-haul should warn, not silently re-price.
  • Order numbers are optional but sticky. One field per item; it's the only string that lets a user reconcile with the agent's site later.
  • Out of stock is a real state. Seller ghosts you constantly. "Unavailable — find a rep" should bounce the item back to the shelf with its W2C intact.

Warehouse

  • Actual weight overwrites the estimate, and the diff is shown. Users learn to estimate better; you learn per-category weights across all users.
  • Storage countdown per item, not per haul. The clock starts at that item's arrival. Show the earliest expiry at haul level.

QC

  • Keyboard-first review. ← → through photos, G / R to decide, auto-advance to the next item. QC 12 items in ninety seconds.
  • Red light demands a reason. The reason chip is what generates the message and what makes seller stats meaningful later.
  • Credenza writes the message, the user sends it. EN + 中文, item number pre-filled, the flagged photo referenced. Never automate the send.
  • QC photos stay attached forever. They become the item's record on the shelf — best receipt in the hobby, and the thing people screenshot.
  • Seller reputation falls out for free. "This seller: 12 green, 2 red across your shelf." Later, across everyone's.

Parcel & after

  • Chargeable = max(actual, volumetric). Show both, always. Let the divisor be edited — agents use 5000 and 6000.
  • Rates are the user's numbers with a date stamp. Never pretend to know today's line prices. "Rates you entered Jul 12."
  • Advice, not just arithmetic: headroom to the next weight break, cost of splitting, what removing the heaviest item saves, what dropping shoe boxes saves.
  • A parcel is an object. A haul can have A, B, C. Items not in a parcel are visibly in storage, with the clock running.
  • Declared value: one field, one flat warning. No advice, no optimisation. State the user's own threshold and stop.
  • Received closes the loop. Fit answer per item feeds the size recommendation. Landed cost replaces the goods-only total.
